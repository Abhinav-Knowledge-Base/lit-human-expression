# The Rhythm of Her Gravity

I have become a scholar of your gravity,
a quiet keeper of the small galaxies you wear.

I know them all—
the geometry of how you frame your face.

There are the long ones, slender and sharp,
and the delicate, thin ones that catch the light when you laugh.
There are the small rounds, hiding quietly beneath your hair,
and the grand, sweeping circles that demand the room's attention.

The tiny, breathless studs—almost invisible,
yet impossible to ignore.

Sometimes, you choose the ones that match your dress,
a perfect, calculated harmony.

But my favorites are the ones that rebel—
the ones that outshine the fabric, outshine the room,
and make everything else fade into background noise.

And then, the architectural wonders:
Those long, majestic ones that frame your neck
like the proud minarets on either side of the Taj Mahal—
holding up a monument of absolute beauty.

And the round, intricate ones, heavy and cascading
like a jhumar hanging from the center of a palace ceiling,
shattering the light into a thousand pieces
every time you turn your head.

The world watches your eyes,
or listens to the words you speak.
But I am caught in the swinging pendulum of your ears—
counting the endless ways you choose to conquer me,
one jhumka at a time.
