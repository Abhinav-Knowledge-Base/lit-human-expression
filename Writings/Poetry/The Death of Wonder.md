# The Death of Wonder

Language was only developed to speak of Love—
a currency of pure wonder,
meant for unnecessary laughter,
and the holy, breathless magic of a surprise.

But the world grew loud, and heavy, and cold,
and on that day, I killed the child inside me.

I traded the mystery for a magnifying glass.
I started seeking the meaning of everything,
turning beautiful secrets into problems to be solved.

The presence of that star—the one
that used to guide my dreams—
suddenly stopped making sense
unless I could calculate its distance,
its fire, its end.

I became a collector of answers,
a hunter of symmetry and logic.

I started solving puzzles, finding solutions,
measuring, judging, and comparing the world
until the color bled out of it.

Now, I introspect until the joy turns to dust.
I analyze the breeze instead of letting it touch my skin.
I critique the silence instead of listening to what it holds.

We think that growing up means finding the truth.

But looking back at the quiet graveyard
of my innocence, I realize
the child already knew the only truth that mattered:

The universe isn't a puzzle to be solved.
It was a song to be felt.
